What I found on sites that were already live.

Reported. Never fixed. Later, exploited.

01 · School
I found an XSS flaw on their site and reported it with steps to fix it. The report went unanswered.
What happened
Some time later, someone used that same flaw to inject ads that every visitor saw.
Why I tell this story
It's why a retest comes with everything I do. Finding the flaw is half the job; the other half is proving it's gone.
  1. 02

    I got in through the login page

    Field survey company

    The login had no attempt limit, and common passwords got me in. The password reset code could be guessed too.

    The worst part: every reset code they sent cost them money. A simple script could run up their bill without ever logging in (denial of wallet).

    • Authentication
    • Brute force
    • Denial of wallet
  2. 03

    A contact form that told no one

    Real estate developer

    A handful of requests could knock the contact form offline.

    What hurt more: while testing it we saw that inquiries weren't reaching anyone. They were losing buyers without knowing it.

    • Layer 7 DoS
    • Forms
  3. 04

    A checkout that doubled as a stolen-card tester

    Online store

    Anyone could use their payment page to check in bulk whether stolen or generated cards still worked. That's called card testing, or carding.

    Left unfixed: chargebacks, fees and, eventually, the payment processor freezing the account.

    • Payments
    • Carding
